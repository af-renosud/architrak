Your observation of the French construction industry's billing workflows is spot-on. In French law, once a *Devis* (estimate/quote) is signed with the mention *"Bon pour accord"*, it becomes a legally binding contract (*contrat commercial* or *marché de travaux*). However, administrative practices vary depending on company size, public vs. private domain, and project scale.

Here is an analysis of your three observed workflows, followed by the structured table for your application logic.

### Contextual Elucidation of the 3 Modes

* **Mode 1 (Formal Corporate / ERP Flow):** Common in larger companies or formal project structures. The extra *Commande* step (often a *Bon de commande* or formal contract countersignature) serves internal ERP compliance to generate a firm order in their accounting system before work begins. Progress is validated via a signed *Situation de travaux* (progress billing state) before issuing the definitive *Facture de situation*.
* **Mode 2 (Standard Construction Practice):** The most widespread method for mid-sized construction projects (*BTP*). The signed *Devis* acts as the sole contract. As phases finish, the contractor submits a *Situation* detailing percentage completion (often reviewed/validated by you, the architect, as *Maître d'œuvre*). Once validated, the corresponding *Facture* is generated.
* **Mode 3 (Direct / Simplified Flow):** Standard for smaller works, single-trade contractors (*artisans*), or fixed-phase jobs (e.g., deposit + balance). The company bypasses formal progress tracking (*Situations*) and bills fixed percentages or milestones directly via *Facture d'acompte* (deposit invoice) or final *Facture*.

| Workflow Mode | Initial Contract Phase | Progress Validation Phase | Invoicing Phase | Typical Context / Use Case |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mode 1** | `Devis` → `Bon de Commande` | `Situation de Travaux` (Signed) | `Facture de Situation` | Large companies, strict internal ERP workflows, formal commercial contracts. |

| **Mode 3** | `Devis` (Signed) | *None* | `Facture` (Acompte / Solde) | Small projects, *artisans*, quick turnarounds, or simple milestone billing. |

### Technical Notes for Application Logic

1. **Legal Status of Devis:** Under Code Civil Art. 1103, a signed *Devis* serves as a contract. In Mode 1, a `Commande` is an internal or administrative duplication of this commitment, not a legal requirement to replace the signed quote.
2. **Situation vs. Facture:** A *Situation de travaux* is legally an **extracontractual draft statement** of work completed, not an accounting invoice. Legally, tax and VAT (TVA) obligations in France are triggered only when the official **Facture** is generated against that situation.
3. **Sequential Linking:** In your application schema, `Situation` records should ideally maintain a parent-child relationship with the original `Devis` ID, and each generated `Facture` should reference both its `Devis` ID and its corresponding `Situation` batch number (e.g., *Facture de Situation N°1*).
