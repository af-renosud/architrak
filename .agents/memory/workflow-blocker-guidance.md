---
name: Workflow blocker guidance
description: Explain blocked actions at the point of use without weakening workflow safeguards.
---
Blocked workflow actions must explain why, identify the prerequisite, and offer a direct route to the relevant field or section when one exists. Support keyboard and touch, not just hover.

**Why:** The user found inactive stage buttons unintelligible and approved contextual help, persistent action-specific checklists and targeted highlighting.

**How to apply:** Keep approval prerequisites separate from sending prerequisites. Distinguish missing input, waiting, read-only restrictions and technical failures. Never treat an unavailable prerequisite check as clearance. Preserve drafts, retain server validation, and never automatically execute an action when its last blocker clears.

The architect may explicitly authorize signing despite unresolved contractor questions, without resolving or dismissing those questions.

**Why:** The user stated that outstanding questions are not always material to signing and that the architect should decide.

**How to apply:** Keep the default question blocker; require deliberate authorization scoped to the current questions, retain an audit reason and identity, and preserve all other signing safeguards.

Next-action guidance must distinguish architect actions from waiting on the client. AI may explain verified facts, but must never determine payment eligibility, invent deadlines, or treat a certificate as proof of payment.

**Why:** The user approved prominent next-action flags to reduce visual clutter, especially for opening deposits, without accidental financial actions.

**How to apply:** Keep links navigational and retain separate generation/send/payment confirmations. Separate draft, actual email delivery, pending payment evidence and ledger coverage. Unknown evidence must not look like clearance or a new payment demand.

Source-dependent correction forms must let architects consult the original PDF while editing, without closing the form.

**Why:** The floating viewer deliberately yields to modal forms to prevent focus stealing; relying on it for required source evidence made correction unnecessarily difficult.

**How to apply:** Place source viewing within the correction dialog, preserve edits across responsive panel switches, and retain global floating-viewer focus protections. Always offer a separate-tab original link without requiring a page number first.

Review-source actions must be visually separate from explanations and audit records; history, evidence and monitoring start collapsed.

**Why:** The user approved the action-first redesign because dense descriptive and historical content obscured quick decisions.

**How to apply:** Keep actual pending approvals and safety warnings visible, retain all evidence behind disclosures, and distinguish recording a review from correcting quotation rows.
