---
name: Certificate reporting reflects issued authorisations
description: Architect responsibility ends at issuing payment authorisations; payment confirmation is optional.
---

Client-facing certificate summaries should report issued payment authorisations, not assert that contractors received payment. Confirmation of receipt is the contractor's responsibility, not a mandatory architect follow-up. Payment confirmations may be recorded when available, but absence of confirmation must not be treated as evidence of non-payment.

**Why:** The user explicitly stated that reliable contractor receipt information is not routinely available and that chasing every payment creates an inappropriate burden on the architect.

**How to apply:** Distinguish issued/certified amounts from confirmed receipts. Do not require confirmation to progress ordinary reporting, label unconfirmed amounts unpaid, or infer receipt from issuance. Preserve existing payment evidence; this policy is not permission to delete ledger records.

Use contractor-level totals for the certificate authorisation summary when quotation allocation is not proven; list the signed quotation references without inventing allocations.

**Why:** Historical manual and multi-quotation certificates do not always carry a complete per-quotation allocation. Tax evidence alone cannot establish one. Summing issued period authorisations once per contractor is defensible; adding invoice totals again or summing cumulative works would double-count.

**How to apply:** Keep invoice-based dashboard measures distinct from the issued-authorisation PDF summary. Preserve sealed PDFs and use controlled reissue for document corrections.
